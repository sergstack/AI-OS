`[Thinking]` applies these lenses to real decisions. `[Thinkers OS]` remains responsible for authors, corpus, source intake, artifact provenance, synthesis maintenance, and status changes.

## Precedence

1. Case facts and direct evidence.
2. Project Instructions and governance.
3. Project-specific rules.
4. Validated synthesis patterns.
5. Active provisional synthesis patterns.
6. Isolated Judge-pass author patterns.
7. Candidate and archival material.

Thinker patterns cannot override facts, routing, governance, project instructions, or project boundaries.

## Selection and anti-bloat rules

1. First classify one primary problem type from the table below.
2. For a material complex case, select its two primary lenses.
3. Add the optional third lens only when the case contains its trigger.
4. A fourth lens requires a written reason tied to a distinct material risk.
5. Never activate more than four lenses; prefer two or three.
6. For a simple, routine, reversible task, do not activate a synthesis pattern merely to add conceptual depth.
7. Source authors establish provenance; they are not mandatory active lenses for every application.
8. Do not enumerate unrelated authors. Record only selected lenses and materially plausible exclusions.

## Lens boundaries

| Lens | Use when | Do not use when | Transfer risk | Route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clausewitz Friction | consequential plans have material uncertainty or dependencies | routine reversible work or adversarial characterization of colleagues | militarized framing; analysis as delay | `[Thinking]` |
| Deming Process | recurring outcomes have an uncertain cause class | urgent containment or a directly evidenced violation | unsupported statistical labels; accountability dilution | `[Thinking]`; reusable rule candidate to `[AI OS]` |
| Kahneman Framing | high-impact judgment may depend on baseline or equivalent framing | retrospective personal labeling or replacement for domain evidence | slow review assumed correct; false equivalence | `[Thinking]` |
| Lenin Apparatus | rules, permissions, dependencies, or concentration may reproduce outcomes | political advocacy, coercion, violence, or literal revolutionary transfer | ideological overreach; intent inference | `[Thinking]` |
| Ostrom Governance | multiple owners, shared resources, monitoring, or conflict paths matter | simple ownership where governance adds overhead | checklist formalism; fragmentation | `[Thinking]`; reusable rule candidate to `[AI OS]` |
| Forrester Feedback | evidenced feedback, delay, nonlinear effect, or policy side effect matters | unsupported causality or qualitative maps presented as proof | model misspecification; false precision | `[Analytics]` for quantitative claims |
