## Lens Router and anti-bloat

1. Classify one primary problem type.
2. For a material complex case, select two primary lenses.
3. Add an optional third lens only when its trigger is present.
4. A fourth lens requires a written reason tied to a separate material risk.
5. Maximum four; prefer two or three.
6. Do not activate synthesis for a simple, routine, reversible task merely to add depth.
7. Source authors are provenance, not mandatory active lenses.
8. Do not enumerate irrelevant authors; record only selected lenses and materially plausible exclusions.

## Conflict Map

### Central redesign vs polycentric fit

Change only evidenced structural constraints while preserving bounded local autonomy, monitoring, and conflict paths. Avoid both uniform redesign that erases local fit and polycentric language that protects a harmful bottleneck.

Sources: `THINKERS-LENIN-PATTERN-001`, `THINKERS-LENIN-JUDGE-001`, `THINKERS-OSTROM-PATTERN-001`, `THINKERS-OSTROM-JUDGE-001`.

### Act under friction vs model first

Match modeling depth to reversibility, impact, and feedback risk. Use bounded dependency review for reversible action; require deterministic modeling for high-impact delayed intervention.

Sources: `THINKERS-CLAUSEWITZ-PATTERN-001`, `THINKERS-CLAUSEWITZ-JUDGE-001`, `THINKERS-FORRESTER-PATTERN-001`, `THINKERS-FORRESTER-JUDGE-001`.

### System cause vs direct accountability

Keep causal classification separate from accountability design. Contain direct violations, then assign correction and monitoring to the level able to change the cause.

Sources: `THINKERS-DEMING-PATTERN-001`, `THINKERS-DEMING-JUDGE-001`, `THINKERS-OSTROM-PATTERN-001`, `THINKERS-OSTROM-JUDGE-001`.

### Deliberate review vs decision friction

Trigger framing review only when impact, irreversibility, or plausible frame sensitivity crosses a declared threshold. Do not use review as generic delay.

Sources: `THINKERS-KAHNEMAN-PATTERN-001`, `THINKERS-KAHNEMAN-JUDGE-001`, `THINKERS-CLAUSEWITZ-PATTERN-001`, `THINKERS-CLAUSEWITZ-JUDGE-001`.

For every applicable conflict, record the dominating position, case evidence, synthesis boundary, and failure mode. If none applies, record `none`.

## Active provisional synthesis patterns

### `SYN-001-REPEATED-FAILURE-DIAGNOSIS`

- use: recurring failure persists after local fixes or may involve feedback, delay, or unresolved dependencies.
- workflow/output: separate signal/requirement; map recurrence/dependencies/feedback; classify cause; assign bounded action; set verification and rollback.
- risks: unsupported statistical labels, accountability dilution, qualitative loops presented as proof.
- source patterns: Deming, Forrester, Clausewitz Judge-pass patterns.

### `SYN-002-DECISION-UNDER-FRICTION`

- use: consequential decision combines uncertainty, material dependencies, frame sensitivity, or delayed effects.
- workflow/output: state objective/dependencies; record baseline/frames; map evidenced feedback; choose review depth/action; set monitoring and rollback.
- risks: militarized framing, unsupported bias labels, false model precision, analysis paralysis.
- source patterns: Clausewitz, Kahneman, Forrester Judge-pass patterns.

### `SYN-003-OWNERSHIP-AND-SYSTEM-CHECK`

- use: failure involves ambiguous ownership, interacting rules, concentrated permissions, or multiple decision centers.
- workflow/output: separate signal/cause/accountability; map owners/rules; test concentration/local autonomy; assign action/safeguards/rollback.
- risks: accountability dilution, governance sprawl, coercive or ideological transfer.
- source patterns: Deming, Ostrom, Lenin Judge-pass patterns.

### `SYN-004-BIAS-AWARE-DECISION-REVIEW`

- use: high-impact decision may change with baseline, equivalent framing, or unverified dependency assumptions.
- workflow/output: predeclare threshold/baseline; capture initial judgment; restate equivalent frames; check evidence; record reversal/revisit.
- risks: hindsight accusation, post-hoc baseline, slow review assumed superior, false equivalence.
- source patterns: Kahneman and Clausewitz Judge-pass patterns.

### `SYN-005-REVERSIBLE-INTERVENTION-GATE`

- use: structural/policy intervention has uncertain feedback, multiple owners, concentrated dependencies, or costly reversal.
- workflow/output: define outcome/evidence; map feedback/governance; route quantitative claims to Python/SQL validation; pilot; predeclare stop/rollback.
- risks: false precision, governance overbuild, coercive transfer, unsupported system labels.
- source patterns: Forrester, Ostrom, Lenin, Deming Judge-pass patterns.

## Available isolated Judge-pass author patterns

These patterns are available only at precedence level 6. They do not change the Lens Router, are not members of the five-pattern active synthesis set, and should be selected only when the case trigger is material.

### Bounded Decision Design — Herbert A. Simon

- use: a decision is overloaded by alternatives, unclear premises, limited attention, or a disputed problem representation;
- apply: define mandatory constraints and aspiration thresholds; separate facts from values and guesses; test the representation; search a bounded option set; choose a reversible sufficient action; reopen when feedback violates a threshold;
- do not use: to lower mandatory standards, automate value choices, or ignore safety, rights, domain evidence, or irreversible downside;
- transfer risk: satisficing can rationalize low standards and decomposition can hide important interactions;
- evidence/status: complete P0/P1 corpus; Judge pass; isolated `active_provisional`; owner acceptance pending; production not authorized.

### Constraint-First Flow Review — Eliyahu M. Goldratt

- use: recurring flow delay, too many concurrent initiatives, suspected bottlenecks, project multitasking, or persistent operational conflict;
- apply: define the system and guardrails; verify the current constraint; exploit before elevating; subordinate release and priorities; limit multitasking; monitor buffers; test conflict assumptions; rediagnose after change;
- do not use: when the system boundary, goal, demand, constraint evidence, safety, quality, rights, or governance guardrails are unknown;
- transfer risk: a single-constraint framing can erase plural goals, while estimate compression and subordination can externalize harm;
- evidence/status: complete P0/P1 corpus; Judge pass; isolated `active_provisional`; owner acceptance pending; production not authorized.
